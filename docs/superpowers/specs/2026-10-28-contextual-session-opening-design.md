# Contextual session opening — Design

**Date:** 2026-10-28
**Status:** Approved 2026-10-28
**Builds on:** PR #21 (`fix/voice-echo-and-cold-start`: the graph summary sent up front, the mic gate while a clip plays, the cold-start work) and the close-session Lambda (LM-15, deployed 2026-10-28).
**Scope:** the iOS app (Flutter), the close-session Lambda (`infra/lambda/close-session`), and Supabase. The web app is out of scope.

## 1. Intent

Lumen should greet a returning user the way a friend picks up where you left off: "Hey, how are you? How did the beta launch go?" It should not reintroduce itself every time.

Today every new notebook opens with the same bundled clip, "Hi, I'm Lumen. What's on your mind today?" (`assets/audio/lumen_opening.wav`, 3.45 s). The clip plays the instant you tap, while the Gemini connection sets up (about 0.7 s) behind it. That instant start is the result of the cold-start work, and it must not regress.

The user's decisions:
- Lumen introduces itself **only the first time** someone uses it.
- Every later session opens with **a greeting and one relevant starter question**. The question comes from things left open (open questions, plans with dates, things they said they'd do) or other relevant context from their memory across sessions.
- **The whole greeting is in Lumen's voice.** No split between a recorded "hey" and a live question.
- **A session brief is prepared as soon as a session ends**, and that same brief is used when the next session starts.
- **Lumen is ready ahead of time from sign-in**, so a new session starts with no wait.

## 2. The approach (decided)

When a session closes, the close-session step does three things:
1. writes the closing card, as today;
2. writes a **session brief**: what's most relevant to this person right now, and the one thread to open with;
3. writes the **opening line** and renders it as audio in Lumen's voice (Gemini TTS, voice `Aoede`, the same voice and method used for today's bundled clip, see `tool/generate_opening_audio.mjs`).

At sign-in, alongside the Gemini token and audio player it already prepares, the app downloads the latest brief and its opening audio. When the user starts a new notebook, the app plays that clip instantly, exactly as it plays the bundled one today. The live Gemini session connects behind it and is told:
- the exact line it already said;
- the brief, so it can follow up naturally.

A connection held open from sign-in, and a greeting generated by Gemini Live in advance, were both **rejected**. This project tried them in September (commits `ba3642f` and `ab39ede`, and `docs/voice-session-lifecycle-plan.md`). They billed a generated greeting on every warm-up (most never heard) and needed keep-alive and refresh logic. This design renders **one opening per session close**, and it's almost always heard next time.

## 3. Behaviour

### 3.1 Which opening plays

| Situation | Opening |
|---|---|
| The user's first session ever (no closed session with anything said by the user) | The bundled intro clip, unchanged: "Hi, I'm Lumen. What's on your mind today?" |
| Returning user, a brief with ready audio is available | The brief's opening clip, played instantly |
| Returning user, no usable brief (none yet, failed to render, or failed to download) | Fallback (§3.4) |
| Continuing an existing notebook (Talk/Type from the closing card, or reopening one) | No greeting. Unchanged from today: the session resumes with context |

"First session" is per **account**, not per device. A new phone must not reintroduce Lumen to someone who has used it before.

**How "returning" is decided (my call):** an account is returning if the server shows *any* sign of earlier use:
- a notebook with `entity_count > 0`;
- any user turn in `conversation_turns`;
- any `session_cards` row.

This also catches people who used Lumen before this ships, or whose closes failed. The app fetches the flag at sign-in along with the brief, and caches it on the device once true. If the flag is still unknown when the user taps (new phone, offline, lookup not back yet), the **fallback clip** plays, not the intro. The one exception is an account created during this app session through sign-up, which is known to be new and gets the intro. Starting never waits for the lookup.

**Modes (my call):** today only Just talk exists in the app, and only Just talk produces cards. In v1 the brief-driven opener applies to new **Just talk** notebooks. Once LM-02/LM-03 ship, other modes keep their own planned openers, and this design doesn't replace LM-03. A later mode's closing may also update the brief once its generator exists (LM-36). The first-time intro is today's clip until LM-25 (first-session onboarding) replaces it.

**One play per brief (my call):** an opener is used **once**. The app records that it played (server-side, so other devices see it too). After that, until a new brief is written, returning users hear the fallback clip. That covers sessions that produce no new brief: leaving without speaking (`422 nothingToSummarize`), a failed close, or the app killed mid-session. Otherwise Lumen would ask the same question again. A brief older than **21 days** is also treated as unusable, since the thread is likely stale. The live prompt is told whether the brief's thread was already raised in an earlier session.

### 3.2 What a good opening sounds like

- **Shape:** a warm hello, then a question about **one** thread, then Lumen listens. A short, natural follow-up about the same thread is fine ("How's the marathon coming along? Is the knee holding up?", the user's preference, 2026-10-31). One or two sentences, about 3–6 s spoken.
- **The thread comes from the conversation that just ended.** Earlier threads stay in the brief's focus as background but don't open the next session unless the person raised them again (verified 2026-10-31: answered and ignored threads both stop after one session).
- **Thread choice, in priority order:**
  1. something left open: an unresolved question, a plan with a date ("ship the beta by Friday"), or something they said they'd do;
  2. failing that, the most relevant recent thread from memory.
- **Feels like a friend catching up:** "Hey, how's it going? Did the Atlas beta make it out on Friday?"
- **It is not:** a recap of last session, a list, a reintroduction, or database-sounding phrasing ("I remember you said…", "According to my notes…").
- **Tense-safe.** The line may be heard hours or weeks later, so it never assumes a planned event has or hasn't happened ("How are things with the marathon?", not "How did the marathon go?"), and uses no dates or day names. The brief writer is told the conversation's date.

### 3.3 Sensitive topics (confirmed)

A journal holds hard things: grief, health scares, breakups. **Confirmed by the user:** the opener never leads with a painful topic. That includes loss, illness, conflict, or anything the person was clearly upset about, *unless* the person framed it as something they want to follow up on ("I'll tell you how the scan goes"). In that case the opener may ask about it gently. If no safe thread exists, Lumen falls back to a warm, open greeting with no specific question.

### 3.4 Fallback for returning users

If no brief is usable, a returning user hears a **second bundled clip** in Lumen's voice, rendered the same way as today's intro. For example: "Hey, good to see you again. What's on your mind today?" The words are the builder's call; it must not say "I'm Lumen". This keeps the instant start and never reintroduces Lumen. (My call: the alternative, a live greeting from Gemini, adds roughly 1.3–1.5 s of silence.)

### 3.5 After the opening

The live session's system prompt (`SESSION OPENING` in `lib/logic/conversation_policy.dart`) currently names the bundled line as the greeting. It must instead state **the exact line that was played**, whichever clip it was, and include the brief in the up-front context next to the graph summary. Lumen must never greet again or repeat the question. Its first reply responds to what the user says, and it can follow the thread the opener raised.

Gemini 3.1 only uses context sent **before its first reply** (verified by the session that built PR #21). So everything Lumen needs about the brief must go in the setup or up-front context. Nothing about the brief can be sent later in the session.

## 4. The session brief

### 4.1 Contents

One current brief **per user**: the most recent close wins. It holds:
- `focus`: a few short lines on what's most relevant to the person right now, across their memory, not just the last session. Lumen uses this as context in the next session.
- `openingThread`: the one thread the opener asks about, and why. Lumen uses this to follow up.
- `openingLine`: the exact words of the opener.
- the **opening audio** (PCM16 mono, 24 kHz, the format `StandardOpening` already parses), or a reference to it;
- `sourceNotebookId`, `createdAt`, and a version.

### 4.2 What it's written from

The brief should reflect "most relevant to the user right now", not only the session that just ended. Inputs to the model:
- this session's transcript (already loaded for the card);
- the **previous brief** (so open threads carry forward until they're resolved);
- open items from memory: unanswered `follow_ups`, `entities.questions`, and the most-mentioned people and topics across notebooks (`user_entities`, from LM-06).

**Commitments in v1 (my call):** nothing stores "things they said they'd do" yet. `follow_ups` holds questions *Lumen* planned to ask, and LM-08's `open_items` isn't built. So in v1 the model finds plans and commitments by reading the transcript, and they carry forward through the previous brief until they're resolved. This doesn't depend on LM-08. When `open_items` lands, it becomes another input.

### 4.3 Where it's produced

It's produced in the close-session flow. **The card must not wait for the brief or the audio.** The closing card already takes about 4 s, and rendering speech adds a few more. How to split it (same model call as the card plus a separate audio step, a second Lambda invocation, an async job, and so on) is **left to the builder**, within these constraints:
- the closing card's latency and failure behaviour don't change;
- the brief and audio are best-effort. If they fail, the previous brief stays, and if there is none, the fallback clip plays;
- the existing deadline (Lambda timeout 30 s, `defaultDeadlineMs` 27 s) is respected.

The close can run more than once per notebook (leaving, then the card screen retrying, then leaving again). Producing the brief more than once for the same notebook revision must be harmless.

### 4.4 Storage and access

- Owner-only reads with the user's own session (RLS, like `session_cards`).
- **Writes follow the `save_session_card` pattern (my call):** a SECURITY DEFINER function called by the Lambda with the user's own token. No service-role key and no new secret. That means a signed-in user could technically overwrite their own brief, which is harmless: it's their data and only affects their own opener. The pgTAP tests check owner-only reads and that the table can't be written directly.
- **The audio is stored in a table, not Supabase Storage (my call):** a `bytea` column (or similar) on the brief row, with only the current clip kept per user (about 150–300 KB). It's removed automatically by the `auth.users` cascade when the account is deleted. Storage would need cleanup code in `delete-account`, and that function's source isn't on `main`.
- Marking an opener as played (§3.1) is a small owner-callable function on the same row.

## 5. Ready from sign-in

- At sign-in, and when the app returns to the foreground, the app fetches the current brief and its audio, next to what `VoiceServicesScope` already prepares (the credential, the audio player, and the turn-journal warm-up). The clip is decoded and held in memory, ready to play.
- After a session closes and its brief is written, the app picks up the new one, so the next session that same day uses it. Polling, a notification, or refetching when the dashboard shows are all acceptable; **the builder's call**.
- Starting a new notebook must never wait on the brief. If it isn't ready yet, the fallback clip plays.
- Nothing about Gemini Live changes: no connection is held open. The credential prefetch stays as it is.

## 6. Non-goals

- The web app's opening. The web app doesn't use the brief.
- Showing the brief or "next time I'll ask about…" on the closing card or anywhere else in the UI. This could be a follow-up; it's not in v1.
- Per-notebook briefs, or more than one opening variant per user.
- Changing what happens when an existing notebook is continued.
- Re-recording or changing the first-time intro clip.

## 7. Anti-goals (would make this a failure even if it technically works)

- Opening a notebook is no longer instant: any silence before Lumen's first word that today's build doesn't have.
- Lumen reintroduces itself to someone who has used it before.
- The opener brings up something painful out of nowhere.
- The opener sounds like a database lookup or a recap.
- The closing card gets slower or less reliable.
- Lumen greets twice, or repeats the opener's question in its first reply.

## 8. Testing expectations

- Close-session: the brief and opening are written from a transcript, the previous brief carries forward, failures leave the card untouched, repeated closes are idempotent. Use the Lambda's existing test setup with injected fetch.
- Database: owner-only access, server-only writes, deletion on account removal (pgTAP, following `session_cards_test.sql`).
- App:
  - opening selection (first-ever, brief ready, no brief, continuing);
  - the `SESSION OPENING` prompt names the line actually played;
  - the brief is in the up-front context;
  - start never waits on the brief.
- On the simulator against the live backend:
  - first session gets the intro;
  - close a session and open a new one: the opener asks about something from the closed session, with no added delay before the first word (compare the `greeting playing` timing log);
  - with the brief unavailable, the fallback clip plays.

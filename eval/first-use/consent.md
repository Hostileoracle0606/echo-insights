# Consent (read aloud before starting)

Read the part between the lines to the person word for word, then ask the
question at the end. Do not start until they say yes. You do not collect
a signature, and you do not write down their name anywhere in the study
files.

The wording avoids the words the protocol bans before the three questions
(see `protocol.md`, "Ground rules"), so the consent does not hint at what
the app does. A test checks this. If you change the wording, keep it that
way.

---

Thanks for doing this. Before we start, here is what will happen and what
happens to it afterwards.

**What this is.** I am testing an app I built, not testing you. If
something is confusing, that is a problem with the app, and finding those
problems is the point.

**What I will do.** I will give you three short things to try, 30 to 40
minutes in all. I will watch, time things, and write down what you do:
for example, how long something took, where you got stuck, and whether
you finished. I will also ask you a few questions about the app.

**What you say to the app.** You will be talking to the app about your
day. Please do not share anything real or private: make things up or
stay vague if you like. What you say and type goes to Google's Gemini
service so the app can understand and answer you, and Google may hold on
to it under its own terms, which I do not control. On my side, you use a
test account that I erase when we finish. I do not write down what you
tell the app, and I do not film you or tape this.

**What I write down, and who sees it.** I write down what you did, not
what you said, under a code like "P2", never your name. Those records go
into a code repository that other people can read, for example people
interested in my work. They show things like "2 of 4 people finished the
first task".

**Quotes.** If you say something about the app that I would like to use,
I will jot it down and, at the end, read you the exact words. I only use
a quote if you say yes to that exact wording; otherwise I cross it out.
You can say no without giving a reason.

**Stopping.** You can stop at any time, skip any task or question, and
you do not need to say why. If you stop, I will ask whether you want me to
throw away what I wrote about your session, and I will do what you say.

**Do you have any questions? Are you happy to go ahead?**

---

Facilitator: if they say yes, set `"consent": true` on the sheet. If they
say no, thank them and stop; record nothing.
